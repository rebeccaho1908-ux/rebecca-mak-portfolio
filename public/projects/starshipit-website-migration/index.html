Migration, performance & SEO project

Starshipit Website Migration, Performance & SEO Optimisation

Summary

Led the audit and refinement of Starshipit’s website migration from Sanity to Vercel, working across brand, UX, SEO, accessibility, and performance.

My role included identifying differences between the live and migrated sites, fixing bugs and content issues, improving Core Web Vitals, optimising images, resolving SEO and accessibility problems, and documenting workflows for GitHub, Vercel, and Codex.

The result was a faster, more search-friendly, accessible, and scalable website with Lighthouse scores of 97 for Performance and 100 for SEO on desktop.

Programs used:
Codex Google PageSpeed Insights

Challenge

Starshipit was in the middle of a major website migration from Sanity to Vercel while also rolling out a refreshed brand system. The challenge was not just to move the site across platforms, but to make sure the new build matched the live site, fixed legacy issues, improved SEO and accessibility, and maintained brand consistency across every touchpoint.

At the same time, the site had a long list of technical and content issues: broken links, duplicate pages, missing metadata, mixed-domain URLs, missing alt tags, hreflang conflicts, poor image handling, and performance issues affecting Core Web Vitals. The migration also needed clear internal processes, documentation, and training so the team could confidently manage GitHub, Vercel, and AI-assisted workflows going forward.

Solution

As the sole Digital Designer, I worked across design, content, QA, and implementation to help bring the new Starshipit site up to launch standard.

I audited the current website against the Vercel build and recorded inconsistencies across content, layouts, links, navigation, and page behaviour. I also helped establish the migration workflow by setting up GitHub, Vercel, and Codex processes, creating team training notes, and documenting how to test changes, create pull requests, and write effective prompts for AI-assisted development.

Performance and Core Web Vitals. I worked on reducing CLS, improving image delivery, tightening hero image sizing, removing unnecessary font and script overhead, and helping reduce blocking client-side work. I also supported image optimisation by converting eligible JPG and PNG assets to WebP and introducing a process to keep future uploads optimised before PRs are merged.

SEO and search visibility. I fixed broken internal links, metadata issues, sitemap and canonical checks, hreflang conflicts, missing headings, and inconsistent domain linking. I also supported implementation of structured data and AI-readability improvements, including schema enhancements and an llms.txt file to make important public pages easier for AI systems and search engines to understand.

Accessibility and compliance. I helped identify and resolve accessibility issues including unlabeled controls, keyboard accessibility gaps, modal focus behaviour, heading hierarchy, touch target sizing, and generic link naming. This brought the site into alignment with WCAG 2.2 AA / ADA standards.

UX, content and component polish. I fixed bugs across key pages, improved mobile layouts, corrected CTA styling and routing, refined case study and blog components, updated branding details like Magento to Adobe Commerce, and documented existing components and editable options in Figma for better handover and consistency.

Results: saved 3-6 month SEO agency work and $4k per month in dev costs.

The result was a faster, more search-friendly, accessible, and scalable website with Lighthouse scores of 97 for Performance and 100 for SEO on desktop. Saved 3-6 months of SEO agency work and $44k/year in web development costs by bringing the website in-house.

Lighthouse scores reached 97 Performance, 93 Accessibility, 92 Best Practices, and 100 SEO on desktop.

Core Web Vitals passed overall, with strong LCP (0.8s) and INP (62ms), while CLS was significantly improved through layout stabilisation work.

Image optimisation reduced total image weight from 805.7MB to 109.0MB, an approximate 86.5% reduction.

Major SEO issues were resolved, including broken links, duplicate content, missing metadata, sitemap issues, hreflang conflicts, and mixed-domain URLs.

Accessibility gaps were closed across navigation, forms, controls, and dynamic components, bringing the site up to WCAG 2.2 AA / ADA compliance.

The team was left with clearer workflows, training documentation, and a repeatable AI-assisted process for shipping updates through GitHub, Vercel, and Codex.
