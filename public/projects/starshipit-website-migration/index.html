Starshipit Website Migration & AI-Powered Growth Engine

Summary

As Starshipit’s sole Digital Designer, I helped turn the company website from a CMS that was difficult to maintain into a faster, more flexible platform the marketing team could improve in-house.

My role went well beyond visual design. I worked across UX, content, SEO, accessibility, performance, analytics QA and implementation, reviewing the migrated site, fixing issues, shipping improvements and building repeatable workflows for the team.

In the two weeks after launch, visits increased 5.9%, pageviews 9.6%, online contacts 26.7% and online customers 10.8%.

The project also reduced total website image weight by 86.5%, lifted desktop Lighthouse performance to 97 and contributed to an estimated $44,000 per year reduction in external web development costs.

Programs used:
Codex Google PageSpeed Insights

The challenge

Starshipit’s website is an important acquisition channel. It helps eCommerce retailers discover the brand, understand the product and move towards a trial, demo or purchase.

But the existing setup had become slow to work with and heavily dependent on external development support.

During the migration from Sanity to a GitHub and Vercel-based setup, I found issues across content, page layouts, links, metadata, accessibility, image delivery, Core Web Vitals and SEO.

Getting the new website live was only part of the job.

The bigger opportunity was to create a site that performed better and could be improved continuously by a lean marketing team, without every change turning into a lengthy external development project.

My role

As the sole Digital Designer on the project, I worked across the website from audit through to implementation.

My responsibilities included:

  • Auditing the migrated website against the existing live site
  • Reviewing layouts, content, navigation and responsive behaviour
  • Identifying UX, accessibility and visual inconsistencies
  • Investigating and resolving technical SEO issues
  • Improving Core Web Vitals and page performance
  • Optimising the image library and image delivery
  • Reviewing forms, tracking and conversion journeys
  • Improving internal linking and content discoverability
  • Working directly in GitHub, Vercel and Codex
  • Creating repeatable workflows for future website improvements
  • QAing changes before merge and deployment

Rather than stopping at mockups and handing work over, I was able to follow problems through from identification to implementation and QA.

Protecting the migration

My first priority was making sure the migration did not leave customers or search engines with a worse experience.

I reviewed the new site against the previous version, checking URLs, page layouts, navigation, links, metadata, forms and responsive behaviour.

That uncovered issues including broken links, duplicate content, missing metadata, incorrect heading structures, mixed-domain URLs and inconsistent page behaviour.

I also reviewed the parts of the site most closely tied to measurement and conversion, including HubSpot forms, analytics, consent behaviour and key conversion paths.

The approach was simple: reach parity first, then improve.

Performance and Core Web Vitals

Performance became one of the biggest areas of the project.

I worked through Lighthouse and Core Web Vitals issues including image sizing, layout shift, font loading and unnecessary front-end overhead.

The image library was one of the clearest opportunities.

I built an optimisation process that reduced total website image weight from 805.7MB to 109MB, an 86.5% reduction.

Desktop Lighthouse Performance reached 97, while SEO and accessibility reached 100 on key checks.

I also documented a repeatable PageSpeed review process so performance could be checked after future deployments rather than treated as a one-off launch task.

SEO, AEO and discoverability

I worked across technical SEO and content discoverability both before and after launch.

This included fixing broken links, metadata issues, sitemap problems, heading inconsistencies, hreflang conflicts and gaps in internal linking.

I also explored how Starshipit’s content could be structured more clearly for AI search and answer engines.

That work included adding concise answer-first summaries, improving content structure, strengthening internal links and creating routes that made high-value pages easier for crawlers to access.

Across this work:

  • Semrush site health reached approximately 91%
  • AI readability/AEO improved from 68 to 95
  • 72 broken links were fixed
  • 242 answer-first summaries were added
  • 131 high-value pages were pre-rendered for AI crawlers
  • 453 contextual internal links were added

Search click-through rate increased from 1.5% to 2.4%.

Changing how website work gets done

One of the most useful outcomes of the project was a better way of working on the site.

I began using Codex alongside GitHub and Vercel to investigate issues, make changes and prepare them for review.

That meant a problem identified in Semrush, Lighthouse or Search Console could move from investigation to a branch, pull request, Vercel preview, QA and deployment without automatically becoming an external development job.

I created and documented workflows for:

  • PageSpeed optimisation
  • Semrush issue triage
  • Broken-link fixes
  • Internal linking
  • Blog and AEO summaries
  • New page creation
  • Pre-launch readiness checks

AI was useful because it reduced repetitive implementation work. It did not replace judgement.

Brand, UX, messaging, conversion and tracking still needed human review, so I built those checks into the workflow rather than treating generated code as finished work.

Results

The project improved commercial performance, website quality and the team’s ability to maintain the site.

Commercial impact

Comparing the two weeks immediately before and after launch:

  • Visits increased 5.9%
  • Pageviews increased 9.6%
  • Online contacts increased 26.7%
  • Online customers increased 10.8%
  • Search CTR increased from 1.5% to 2.4%

Performance and search

  • 97 desktop Lighthouse Performance
  • 100 Lighthouse SEO on key checks
  • Approximately 91% Semrush site health
  • AI readability/AEO improved from 68 to 95
  • Website image weight reduced by 86.5%
  • Core Web Vitals passed overall

Operational impact

The new workflow made it possible for me and the wider marketing team to investigate and ship website improvements much faster.

It also reduced our reliance on external development support, contributing to an estimated $44,000 per year saving in web development costs.

The more important change, though, was that the migration left Starshipit with a practical system for maintaining and improving the website after launch rather than another rebuild that would gradually become harder to change.

What I learned

This project changed how I think about digital design.

Some of the most valuable improvements were not visual. Site speed, accessibility, search visibility, content structure, measurement and the systems behind the website all shaped the customer experience.

It also changed how I use AI in my work.

The biggest benefit did not come from asking AI to produce finished work. It came from using it inside a clear process: identify a problem, investigate it, implement a change, review the result and keep the parts that worked.

That combination of design judgement, technical understanding and stronger workflows allowed me to contribute well beyond the boundaries of a traditional website design role.
